# Scope: character creator level 2, hair first (roadmap R15)

Read-only scoping pass (Nov 2026) from the code and assets, not play-tested. Items marked (inference) were not verified.

## How the sprite is built today
- One prebuilt PNG per character and facing: 85 frames of 64x80 (5440x80), about 33 KB each; all 94 character PNGs together are about 3.2 MB.
- `<name>_<facing>_hands.json` holds 85 entries `[hand x, hand y, ear x, ear y]`; hair does not affect it.
- `characters/<name>.tres` (SpriteFrames) is generated by `make_godot.py` with 340 AtlasTexture sub-resources per character.
- Hair is the top still layer. `anim.lua` `E.render` renders every layer separately for each pose, so a hair-only image per frame already exists inside the engine, aligned with the head by construction (`headBlit`: shift, `hrot` rotation about the neck, death-frame body rotation, the white hurt flash).
- The head moves relative to the body in almost every frame (bob, crouch, lean, run, hurt, death, type, dance), so a separate hair layer needs a pre-rendered per-frame sheet, not a fixed offset.

## Options and recommendation
- **A. Second AnimatedSprite2D** synced to the body: most code and most room for drift (backwards `sitdown` playback, one-shots, `pause()` while jumping, `_switch_character`), and three places build bare sprites. Not recommended.
- **B. Bake every combination:** about 33 KB per style per facing; multiplies when shirts and pants arrive. Not recommended.
- **C. Runtime-composited single sheet (recommended).** Ship a body-without-hair sheet per facing and one hair-only sheet per style per facing. `Look.frames_for(style)` composites them once (`Image.blend_rect`, `ImageTexture`) and rebuilds a `SpriteFrames` in code with the same regions and durations. Still one AnimatedSprite2D, so sync, held items and the headset are unchanged, and the Look palette shader still runs on the result. Only three call sites change (`player.gd` sprite_frames load, `npc.gd`, `charselect.gd` preview). One cached `SpriteFrames` per style, shared by the player, NPC copies and the preview. Cost: a few ms the first time a style is used (a hitch worth checking on low-end mobile or web) and about 3.5 MB of RAM per style in use. Bytes: 2 body sheets (about 64 KB) plus about 18 hair sheets for 9 styles (an estimated 4 to 8 KB each) is about 170 to 210 KB. Golden test: compositing `bare` + `side` must equal today's `office_teen_<facing>.png` pixel for pixel.

## Art pipeline
- New `tools/build_hair.lua`, modelled on `build_anims.lua`: for each style make a teen still whose Hair layer is the style, run `E.build`, export two sheets by layer visibility (all layers except Hair, and Hair only). Left facings are generated like `make_fem` does.
- Sources: reuse existing Hair layers (site Alan short, office adult receding, casual Garrison curly) remapped to the teen hair colours `9a5124` / highlight `c47a38`; port the make_fem set (long, bob, ponytail, bun, curly); the teen's side-swept hair becomes style `side` (the golden reference); bald needs the Body layer to carry a skull outline (inference: verify with `view_sprite`, otherwise draw a thin scalp style).
- Colours: only hair, its highlight and the outline are safe. A new shade needs a new `Look.SOURCES` row and must stay about 0.06 RGB distance away from the KEEPS colours (brows `6b3416` are close, check). `tests/creator.gd` `_sheet_colors` must scan the hair sheets too. The ponytail tie maps to the shirt colour.
- Verify with `view_sprite`, `lint_sprite`, contact strips of key frames (idle, run, hurt 25 / 28, death, sit / type, dance, call) and `creator_shots.gd`.

## Integration
- `Look`: add `hair_style` (default `side`, identical to today; old saves load as `side`), a `HAIR_STYLES` list, validation in `sanitize()`; `to_dict` / `save_settings` carry it; in `randomize_look` draw the style after the existing draws so seeded colours do not change. `Look.apply` must take a look dict (NPC copies).
- Create step UI: add a `< STYLE >` row (Left / Right cycle) after the name row; wide 368x128 can take one more row, h and v have room; shirts, pants and shoes styles will not fit wide, so plan two pages (LOOK, CLOTHES). Mouse hit rects need their own kind.
- Tests: every style plays all 85 frames in both facings; golden composite test; hair never covers the eyes and stays inside the head box each frame; save and restore of the style; layout audit at wide, h and v.
- Generator lockstep: edit both the `.gd` and the `write()` blocks in `tools/make_godot.py`; CLAUDE.md and ROADMAP R15.

## Steps
1. Pipeline and golden test: bare body and `side` hair sheets, prove `compose == office_teen` (S/M).
2. `Look.frames_for`, the cache, hair style data, switch the three call sites (M).
3. Art: reuse short, receding and curly; port long, bob, ponytail, bun, curly; bald (M/L).
4. UI row, layout audit, save and restore tests (M).
5. Docs (S).

Risks: scalp and outline gaps on bald, left-facing asymmetry, hair over the headset (acceptable, the item draws on top), wide-format row space, a first-use hitch on low-end builds.

## Later parts, same mechanism
- Glasses and beard are as easy as hair (rigid with the head); they need a beard colour role and a glasses KEEP colour.
- Shoes are straightforward (the engine rotates them per leg and keeps the shared boundary column).
- Pants variants must keep the leg gap and the rows 50 to 55 that the engine reads; skirts and shorts need bare legs below the hem (inference).
- Shirts are the hard one: sleeves live in the arm layers with the hands, the far arm is behind the torso and the near arm in front (three sheets: far sleeve, torso, near sleeve), arms rotate about the shoulder with an elbow so every sleeve style is a full arm layer, and long sleeves or hoods change the neckline.
