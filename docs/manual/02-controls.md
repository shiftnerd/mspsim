# CONTROLS

*Every key, every button, and what each one does when you are sitting down.*

---

## THE BASICS

You walk along the floor of a side-on office. Doors, machines, chairs and coworkers each have a little arrow that floats above them
when you are close enough to use them. **Stand next to something, see the arrow, press `E`.** That is about eighty percent of the
game.

> **TIP:** You can see these keys in-game at any time: press `Esc`, choose **CONTROLS** and turn the pages with `Left` / `Right`.

---

## KEYBOARD: STANDING UP

### Moving and using

| ACTION | KEYS | NOTES |
|---|---|---|
| **Walk** | `A` `D` or `Left` `Right` | |
| **Run** | Hold `Shift` | While walking. |
| **Jump** | `Space`, `W` or `Up` | You crouch for a moment, then leap. It is a very office-sized jump. |
| **Duck** | `S` or `Down` (hold) | You stop while ducking. Handy during a nerf fight. |
| **Use, sit, open doors** | `E` | Works on whatever has the arrow over it. |
| **Pick a target** | `Tab` (next), `Shift`+`Tab` (previous) | Moves the arrow to the next thing within reach without walking. See below. |
| **Cell phone** | `P` | Press `P` or `Esc` again to put it away. |
| **Pause, help** | `Esc` or `Pause` | Opens the pause menu. |
| **Mute or restore the sound** | `M` | |
| **Change the picture shape** | `F9` | Cycles `wide`, `h`, `deck`, `v`, `vclose`. |
| **Full screen or window** | `Alt`+`Enter` (`F11` too in a release build) | The game starts full screen. The same as the **DISPLAY** row of SETTINGS. |
| **Hide or show the clock and counters** | `F10` | Handy for filming. |

### People and gestures

| ACTION | KEYS | NOTES |
|---|---|---|
| **Talk** | `E` next to a coworker | |
| **Ask a coworker to follow you** | `C` next to them | Press `C` again to send them back to their day. Somebody who is not speaking to you will shake their head. |
| **Wave** | `Q` | |
| **Sip your drink** | `F` | Only while you are holding one. A cup lasts a few sips, longer when you are sitting. |
| **Dance** | `X` | Press `X` again, move, jump or press `E` to stop. |

> **NOTE:** You pick up a coffee at the machine, a water at the cooler and a drink at the fridge by pressing `E` next to them.
> You then walk around holding it, like a professional.

### The nerf blaster

Take a blaster from the rack in the cubicle area (`E`). It holds a small handful of darts. Press `E` at the rack again to reload.

| ACTION | KEYS | NOTES |
|---|---|---|
| **Fire** | Tap `Z` | A quick dart. |
| **Long shot** | Hold `Z`, then let go | A dotted arc shows where the dart will land. The counter reads `AIMING n%`. Full power takes a bit over a second. |
| **Put it away** | `G` | |
| **Offer a nerf war** | `V` (while holding the blaster) | Ask the room if it wants to. |
| **Pick up a dart** | Walk over it | Darts that miss end up on the floor. |

> **TIP:** Ducking (`S`) while somebody fires at you is exactly as useful as it looks.

> **NOTE:** Darts stick in walls, monitors and furniture. Monitors flicker when hit. Coworkers react in character. Some laugh. One
> files a complaint with his eyebrows.

## KEYBOARD: WHILE SEATED

Walk to a chair and press **`E`** to sit. Most of the keys change meaning while you are sitting down.

| ACTION | KEYS | NOTES |
|---|---|---|
| **Open the computer** | `E` | At a desk with a computer. |
| **Answer a ringing phone** | `E` | At a desk with a ringing phone. Seated, the phone takes the press during its first two rings; after that `E` is the computer again. The line at the top left says which. |
| **Roll your chair** | Hold `Shift` and press `A` / `D` (or the arrows) | The chair speeds up, coasts and slows by itself, stops at walls and desks, and may bump somebody. |
| **Sip your drink** | `F` | Your cup stands on the desk when you sit. |
| **Stand up** | Any move key (`A`, `D`, arrows) or `Space` | At a seat with no computer (the conference room, the couch), `E` also stands you up. |
| **Phone, pause, mute** | `P`, `Esc`, `M` | As usual. |

> **NOTE:** At a desk with a computer, `E` opens the computer. Press a move key to stand up. You will not be trapped in the chair: a
> plain move key always works.

> **TIP:** Somebody else's chair rolls too. Stand next to a seated coworker, hold **`Shift`** and press **`E`**: the chair rolls away
> from you with them in it, and they scoot back after a moment. A friend laughs. Alan does not. Plain `E` still talks to them.

> **TIP:** While you are seated, you will stretch, scratch, yawn and look about on your own. This is called "authentic."

## THE `TAB` SELECTION

Sometimes two things are within reach: a door and a coworker, a monitor and a rubber duck, a row of network ports. `E` normally
picks the closest one. **`Tab`** moves the arrow to the next thing in reach, left to right. **`Shift`+`Tab`** goes back. Walk away
about ten pixels and the game goes back to choosing for you.

When the game chooses, **the thing you face wins** over something a little closer behind your back, and a computer in front of
you wins over a coworker who just happens to stand at the desk (`Tab` still reaches them). Seated in your chair, `E` is always
your Computer, with two exceptions that are meant to interrupt: a desk phone within reach is answered first while it is in
its first two rings (let it ring longer and the press is the Computer again), and somebody who walked over with news for you
is heard before the Computer. **A line at the top left of the screen says what the press will do** (`E ANSWER THE PHONE`,
`E JOHN HAS NEWS`, `E COMPUTER`), so you never have to guess. A gamepad's use button behaves the same and the line shows its
button.

## GAMEPAD

Plug it in and go. A gamepad covers the whole game: walking, using things, every menu, the Computer, the phone and all the
mini-games. Nothing needs the keyboard or the mouse (that is how it is played on a Steam Deck).

### In the office

| ACTION | BUTTON | NOTES |
|---|---|---|
| **Walk** | Left stick, or D-pad left / right | |
| **Run** | Hold `B` | |
| **Jump** | `A`, or D-pad up | |
| **Duck** | D-pad down, or stick down | |
| **Use / sit / open** | `X` | The same as `E`. |
| **Wave** | `Y` | |
| **Sip your drink** | `LB` | |
| **Cell phone** | `Back` / `Select` | |
| **Pause** | `Start` | |
| **Roll your chair** (seated) | Hold `LT` (or `B`) and push the stick or D-pad sideways | The same as `Shift` + `A` / `D`. |
| **Push a coworker's chair** | Hold `B` and press `X` next to their chair | The same as `Shift` + `E`. |
| **Pick the next target** | `RB` | The same as `Tab`. |
| **Next person** (Free Play) | Click the right stick (`R3`) | The same as the `1` to `7` keys. |
| **Free Play menu** | `Start`, then **FREE PLAY MENU** | The mutator, the toolbox, a team outing, the next person: what the keyboard has on the backtick key, `Shift+F10`, `O` and `1` to `7`. |
| **Stand up** (seated) | Move, or press `A` | |
| **Dance** | Push the right stick up | The same as `X`. Again (or walking off) ends it. |
| **Follow me** | Push the right stick down, next to a coworker | The same as `C`. |
| **Mute the sound** | Push the right stick right | The same as `M`. |
| **Change your look** (Free Play) | Click the left stick (`L3`), or `Start`, then **OUTFIT** | The same as `Y`. |
| **Nerf blaster: fire** | `RT` (hold it to lob, let go to fire) | The same as `Z`. |
| **Nerf blaster: offer a war** | `Y` with the blaster in your hand | The same as `V`. |
| **Nerf blaster: give it away** | Push the right stick left | The same as `G`. |
| **Previous target** | `LB` (when you are not holding a drink) | The same as `Shift+Tab`. |

### In menus, cards and screens

| ACTION | BUTTON |
|---|---|
| **Move the highlight** | D-pad or left stick |
| **OK** | `A` |
| **Back** | `B` |
| **A computer app's letter keys** (CRM, Dispatch, Knowledge) | `Y` steps through them (the strip above the taskbar names the one you are on), `X` presses it |
| **Printer: shake, pray, answer** | `LB` / `RB` shake, `Y` prays, `Up` / `Down` mark an answer and `A` gives it |
| **Escape room: a nudge, or the hint of the page game** | `Y` |
| **Any button of the Computer or the phone** | `RB` / `LB` step the cursor from button to button, `A` presses the one it is on. On the Computer the right stick also moves the cursor freely. Moving with the D-pad hands `A` back to the list. |
| **Leave the chair race, the cookout or paintball** | `Back` / `Select` (`B` is a game button there). Everywhere else `B` leaves. |

Every prompt follows your controls: with a gamepad in your hands the hint lines, the tips of the first day, the captions of
the mini-games and the answers on a card show the pad's buttons (`(A)`, `(B)`, `(BACK)` and so on) instead of key names, and a
key or button you remapped shows as what it is now. The switch is immediate: press a pad button and the screen in front of you
changes to the pad's buttons, press a key or move the mouse and it changes back.

The cookout and paintball say how to leave in their hint line: `Esc` on a keyboard, the `Back` / `Select` button on a pad.

### Touch screens

On a touch screen the game draws a stick (bottom left), `A` and `X` buttons (bottom right; `X` turns into `B` while a screen is
open), a **MENU** button (top right), **NEXT** (the next target) and, while you sit, **ROLL** (hold it and push the stick).
They do exactly what the gamepad buttons of the same name do. Anything else on screen can be tapped like a mouse click.

### What still wants a keyboard

Only two extras: putting a **keyboard key** on an action (the remap page; a pad can move its own buttons there), and
typing a **word** as a run seed (the seed page has wheels for the pad). The debug keys are keyboard-only too.

Answering a card or closing a screen with `A` never makes you jump or stand up: the press belongs to the card.

### Steam Deck and other 16:10 screens

On a 16:10 screen (a Steam Deck is 1280 x 800) the game starts in the **DECK** shape, which fills the screen with no black
bars. The **SHAPE** row of the pause menu (or `F9`) still changes it, and your choice is remembered. When the game loses
focus or the machine goes to sleep, it pauses by itself; **SETTINGS** on the title screen has a **DISPLAY** row (the game starts full screen; pick WINDOW there to play in a window) and an **UNFOCUSED** row
(`PAUSE` / `RUN`) if you would rather keep it running in the background. `Alt`+`Enter` switches between full screen and a
window from anywhere. A window opens in the middle of the screen, as large as fits at a whole multiple of the shape.

### Text size and contrast

The game is drawn in a small picture that is scaled up by a whole number, so the letters are as sharp as they are small.
**SETTINGS** on the title screen and the pause menu in a room both have a **TEXT** row: **NORMAL** or **LARGE**. LARGE draws the
same picture in fewer pixels and scales it up one step more, so every letter (and every person) is about a quarter bigger on
the screen: on a Steam Deck the letters go from 20 to 25 screen pixels high. The room shows a little less of the office at a time,
and a few long lines wrap onto a second line. Two screens are the exception: in the portrait shape (`v`) the outing games, and
in the `wide` shape the character creator, keep NORMAL size while they are open, because they do not fit a LARGE picture. The
**CONTRAST** row switches HIGH contrast on: grey hint text turns brighter or darker, the dark plates behind text on the screen
turn solid and the panel borders get a second line. Both rows work with a gamepad (`Left` / `Right` or `A`), apply at once and
are remembered. Neither changes by itself on any screen: NORMAL is what you get until you change it.

## KEYS INSIDE THE LITTLE SCREENS

Most things that take over the screen read the same few keys. When in doubt: **arrows move, `Enter` chooses, `Esc` backs out.**

| SCREEN | KEYS |
|---|---|
| **Your computer** | Mouse for everything. `Up` / `Down` move in lists, `Enter` opens, `Esc` steps back one level, then closes. Details in [Chapter 5](05-your-computer-and-phone.md). |
| **Your phone** | Arrows move, `Enter` opens, `Esc` goes back, `P` closes. Mouse works. |
| **A decision card** | `Up` / `Down` then `Enter`, or press `1` to `4` to choose that option straight away, or click. `Esc` leaves where it is allowed. A card ignores you for a split second when it appears, so you do not choose by accident. |
| **The end-of-day report** | `Enter` or `Space` to continue. |
| **The title and pause menus** | Arrows and `Enter`. See [Chapter 1](01-getting-started.md). |
| **Outings** | Each game has its own keys, listed in [Chapter 6](06-after-work.md). |

## FREE PLAY EXTRAS

These only work in **Free Play** (not during a campaign):

| KEY | WHAT IT DOES |
|---|---|
| `1` to `7` | Swap to one of the stock people: intern, adult, Dan, Alan, Alan on site, Garrison, John. |
| `Y` | Change your outfit (the creator opens over the room). |
| `O` | Take the team out ([Chapter 6](06-after-work.md)). |
| `` ` `` (backtick) | Cycle a "twist" in the world, such as a themed week. The HUD tags it with a `*`. |
| `H`, `K`, `R` | Hurt, fall over and get back up. For filming. |
| `Shift`+`F10` | Open the toolbox ([Chapter 8](08-tips-and-troubleshooting.md#the-free-play-toolbox)). |
